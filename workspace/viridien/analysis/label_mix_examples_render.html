What the 51 lithology columns are made of, and how hard each legend is

Each row of each column has its fill colour matched to that log's own legend swatches. These are estimates only: the one true answer is log06 (the organisers' example CSV).

Read with care. The shares come from a colour estimator, not from labels. It cannot read hatch patterns, it ignores grey fills (see the parsed Gulf example), and it fails outright on the 11 legends it could not parse. Use it to rank difficulty, not as accuracy.

Difficulty per legend family

Score 0-100 = 40% rows only a pattern can name (all of them on 1-bit scans) + 20% ambiguous colour + 10% colour not in legend + 10% many rock types + 8% busy column + 6% tiny swatches + 6% narrow column. Higher is harder.

A third of all column length sits behind a legend the parser cannot read. Of what it can read, ambiguous colours and colours absent from the legend are each larger than any single named rock.

Shale is the largest named rock at 9.5%, and every other named rock is under 2%. So a strategy that only gets Shale right can still look decent on row accuracy.

What this means for the pipeline

1. Fix the Gulf 3-panel legend parser first: it unlocks a third of the data. 2. Add pattern (hatch) matching for 1-bit and mixed legends. 3. Break colour ties with the hatch or the legend text, or with one JEV call per cluster. 4. Treat grey as a real fill colour.
